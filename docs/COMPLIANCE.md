## 2. EU Cyber Resilience Act — Article 14

### What the requirement asks

Since 11 September 2026, manufacturers placing products with digital elements on the EU market must
notify ENISA (and, where relevant, the relevant national CSIRT) when they become aware of an
**actively exploited vulnerability** in their product:

- **24-hour early warning** — initial notification to ENISA within 24 hours of becoming aware.
- **72-hour notification** — full notification, including an assessment of the vulnerability's
  severity and impact, within 72 hours.
- **Final report** — no later than 14 days after a fix or mitigation is available, or — where a
  fix is not available within 14 days — as soon as one is.

(Source: EU Commission CRA reporting page, cited in `PITCH.md`.)

### Scope caveat — important

**Article 14 applies only to *actively exploited* vulnerabilities.** CVE Autopilot does not
currently determine whether a CVE is being actively exploited in the wild. The CVEs addressed in
this run were drawn from a pip-audit scan and a curated advisory; exploitation status was not
verified against CISA KEV, EPSS, or any other exploitation-intelligence source. A human security
analyst must make that determination before CRA Art. 14 reporting obligations are triggered.

With that caveat, the artefacts below would support the reporting obligation *if* any of the CVEs
were confirmed as actively exploited.

## What a human still has to do

Autopilot handles detection, patching, testing, and evidence collection. The following steps require
human judgment and cannot be delegated to the tool:

1. **Determine exploitation status (CRA Art. 14 gate).** Before filing any ENISA/CSIRT report,
   verify whether each CVE is actively exploited in the wild. Check CISA KEV
   (<https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog>), EPSS scores, and threat-intel
   feeds. CRA Art. 14 obligations are triggered only for actively exploited vulnerabilities.

2. **File the ENISA / national CSIRT early-warning notification (24 h).** If exploitation is
   confirmed, a human must submit the Art. 14 early-warning notification to ENISA and the relevant
   national authority. Autopilot's `results.json` and `REMEDIATION.md` provide the required data
   (affected product, CVE IDs, severity, initial awareness timestamp), but submission is a legal act
   that requires human authorisation.

3. **File the 72 h full notification and final report.** Same as above; use the SLA dashboard and
   SBOM diff as supporting evidence. Final-report deadline is 14 days after fix availability
   (2026-09-26 07:31:12 UTC in this run).

4. **Approve the Python ≥ 3.10 runtime upgrade for the `inventory` service.** Three CVEs were
   escalated because `requests 2.33.0` and `urllib3 2.7.0` — the fix versions — require Python ≥
   3.10, while the service currently runs Python 3.9. A human must approve the runtime upgrade,
   schedule it, and then re-run Autopilot to close `CVE-2026-25645`, `CVE-2026-44432`, and
   `CVE-2026-44431`. Until these are closed, the `inventory` service carries 3 known Medium CVEs.

5. **PCI DSS evidence package.** Gather this document, `REMEDIATION.md`, `results.json`,
   `sla-dashboard.html`, the SBOM diff, and the git commit record into the evidence package for
   your QSA. PCI DSS compliance requires the full audit trail; Autopilot produces the artefacts
   but does not submit them.

6. **Re-scan after the Python upgrade.** Once `inventory` is on Python 3.10+, re-run
   `pip-audit -r requirements.txt` and confirm 0 remaining vulnerabilities before closing the
   escalation tickets.
